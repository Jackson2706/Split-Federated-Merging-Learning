\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical split-federated learning reduces device-side computation by assigning consecutive model segments to clients, edges, and a cloud. H-SFP further decouples these tiers: a lower tier transmits class-conditional feature statistics from which the next tier synthesizes training inputs. Its single diagonal Gaussian per class, however, preserves only marginal first and second moments. A multimodal class may therefore be represented by samples in low-density gaps, while repeated aggregation can erase modes contributed within or across clients. We introduce \method{}, a code-grounded extension that communicates class-conditional mixtures of diagonal-Gaussian components. Clients obtain components by seeded clustering with fixed or held-out-likelihood-selected component counts; edges retain components from different clients, train on mixture samples, re-estimate output-space summaries after their nonlinear segment, and greedily merge same-class components subject to component or serialized-byte budgets. We prove exact preservation of mass, mean, and diagonal second moment for every merge, derive packet and computation costs, and state a conditional fixed-model loss bound without claiming optimization convergence. The evaluation protocol separates within-client multimodality, across-client multimodality, encoder-coordinate mismatch, representation fidelity, predictive quality, and communication. Experimental cells remain linked to a reproducible result manifest and are intentionally unfilled until matched runs are completed.
\end{abstract}
