\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper formulated multimode preservation as a communication-constrained problem in hierarchical split-federated learning. MiSFP represents each class by support-weighted diagonal components, retains within- and across-client structure, re-estimates summaries after a nonlinear edge segment, and uses deterministic class-preserving merges to meet caps or serialized-byte budgets. Its merge operator exactly preserves mass and diagonal first/second moments, while the analysis deliberately stops short of claiming density preservation or optimization convergence. The accompanying protocol and generation pipeline separate predictive utility, representation fidelity, coordinate alignment, and resource cost. Completing the registered matched runs will determine when extra components are useful and when they merely encode noise or incompatible feature coordinates.
