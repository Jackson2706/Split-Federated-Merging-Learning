\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper formulated multimode preservation as a communication-constrained problem in hierarchical split-federated learning. MiSFP represents each class by support-weighted diagonal components, retains within- and across-client structure, re-estimates summaries after a nonlinear edge segment, and uses deterministic class-preserving merges to meet caps or serialized-byte budgets. Its merge operator exactly preserves mass and diagonal first/second moments, while the analysis deliberately stops short of claiming density preservation or optimization convergence. The accompanying protocol separates predictive utility, representation fidelity, coordinate alignment, and resource cost. In the three-seed CIFAR-100 evidence, the round-200 analysis places full mixtures above H-SFP in all three partitions. Under the severe non-IID split, K4 improves accuracy by $3.97\pm0.48$ points while adding $5.2\pm0.1\%$ total communication. A byte-matched float16 mixture keeps the gain, and a shared-snapshot control shows that part of the retained source dispersion reflects encoder-coordinate mismatch. The direct $4.91\pm0.52$-point CIFAR-100 MiSFP--H-SFP difference extends the comparison to CIFAR-10, HAM10000, ImageNet-1K, and ISIC-2018 with propagated SDs and explicit provenance. The fidelity evidence is mixed, and direct matched trials on the additional datasets and minority-sensitive metrics remain open.
