\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This manuscript presented \ehsfp, a convergence-aware episodic prototype learning framework for hierarchical split-federated learning. The framework extends \hsfp by treating each edge or cloud invocation as a stateless learning episode driven by class-wise prototype statistics rather than synchronized activation exchange. Its five modules are episodic prototype memory, reliability-aware prototype aggregation, prototype replay consistency, prototype dropout regularization, and an optional residual prototype generator.

The convergence analysis characterizes \ehsfp as a perturbation of tier-wise non-convex stochastic optimization under prototype-induced synthetic distributions. Under the stated smoothness, variance, bounded-drift, bounded-staleness, and participation assumptions, the method reaches a stationary-neighborhood bound whose residual terms reflect stochastic gradients, hierarchical aggregation, prototype variance, stale prototype updates, missing prototype packets, and prototype-induced distributional drift. Each module is tied to one or more of these terms, giving a principled design path for learning under partial and asynchronous prototype arrivals. Empirically, under a matched 60-round, five-seed protocol, \ehsfp improves on \hsfp on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and HAM10000. It stays within 1.81 points of its undisturbed accuracy under prototype dropout and serverless-style disruptions, and it loses 1.5--2.0 points under stale prototypes. Its gains come mainly from replay consistency and low-rank covariance synthesis. Prototype-based training still trails the strongest weight-sharing baselines under this protocol and does not improve segmentation. In the matched 200-round evaluation, E-HSFP improves the reported accuracy endpoints over H-SFP while reducing interval-(C) communication from 10.94 to 10.39~GB. The remaining outlook is to close the baseline gap and study richer prototype distributions and deployment-level system optimization as complementary directions.
